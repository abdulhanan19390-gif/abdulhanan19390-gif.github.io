Three hill roads worth the detour, even without a destination

Not every trip needs a plan. Some roads are the point.

By Meher Iqbal · 7 min read · Travel

Most travel advice assumes you're going somewhere specific. This isn't that. These are three roads that reward the drive itself — routes people take a long way around, on purpose, because the destination was never really the reason.

The switchback with no viewpoint sign

Some roads get a marked lookout point with a car park and a railing. This one doesn't, which is exactly why it's worth taking slowly — there's no single spot where everyone stops, so the view changes gradually instead of arriving all at once. Best driven in the early morning, when the road is empty enough to actually slow down on the bends.

The valley floor route that ignores the shortcut

There's almost always a faster way through a valley — a tunnel, a newer bypass. The older road along the valley floor takes longer and passes through three small villages that the bypass skips entirely. None of them are tourist stops. That's the appeal: shops that serve locals, not passersby, and a pace set by tractors rather than traffic.

The ridge road that only makes sense at the end

This one is disorienting for the first half — it climbs without any clear reward, switching back on itself in a way that feels like it's going nowhere. Then the ridge opens up all at once, and the entire climb reads differently in hindsight. It's the kind of road that's better not photographed in advance, so the last stretch keeps its surprise.

None of these are shortcuts, and none are the fastest way to anywhere in particular. That's rather the point of a hill road: it's one of the few kinds of travel left where getting there slower is the entire plan.
